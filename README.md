# Taxonomic persistence with ecological flexibility in a diet-responsive insect gut microbiome

Abstract:

Diet is a major driver of host-associated microbiome composition, but its effects on the ecological processes and community relationships underlying microbiome organization remain
poorly understood. Using black soldier fly larvae as a tractable model, we integrated a controlled diet experiment with a meta-analysis to determine how the nutritional
environment shapes gut microbiome ecology beyond taxonomic composition. Diet altered community composition, diversity, core-taxon abundance, and inferred assembly processes, with
substrates differing in the relative contributions of drift, dispersal limitation, and homogeneous selection. Across 39 independent datasets, broad diet-associated community
structure remained detectable despite substantial experimental heterogeneity. Association networks revealed that broad diet categories supported distinct architectures differing
in connectivity, compartmentalization, and sensitivity to simulated stochastic taxon loss. Despite these ecological differences, conserved taxa persisted across diets while
shifting in relative abundance, module membership, and association partners, indicating that recurrent gut taxa do not necessarily maintain a conserved ecological context. These
findings indicate taxonomic persistence can coexist with ecological flexibility and that nutritional environments reshape the ecological processes and association structures
organizing host-associated microbial communities.
